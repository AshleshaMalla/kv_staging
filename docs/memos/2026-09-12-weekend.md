# Weekend memo — 2026-09-12

## What we set out to do

Measure every term in the KV-cache fetch-vs-recompute crossover equation
L* = (kv_bytes_per_token / BW - a) / b for Llama-3.1-8B on a single H100 NVL
node (REPACSS cluster), with Hammerspace NFS as the storage tier.

## What is solid

### GPU / NVLink / NUMA topology

Confirmed from nvidia-smi topo and numactl:

- GPU0 (NUMA 1) <--NV12--> GPU1 (NUMA 0): first NVL pair, cross-socket
- GPU2 (NUMA 3) <--NV12--> GPU3 (NUMA 2): second NVL pair, cross-socket
- 4 NUMA nodes, 16 cores each, ~128 GiB DDR5 per node (~503 GiB total)
- 4x ConnectX-7 NICs (mlx5_0 through mlx5_3)
- Single 3.5T SSD (PERC H755 Front, non-rotational)
- All 4 GPUs: 132 SMs, 93.1 GiB HBM each

Source: `data/hw/2026-09-12T10:51:09-05:00_154420_rpg-93-4/`

### NFS read bandwidth

Measured with fio (--direct=1, bs=128k, posixaio, 30s per run, 16x1G real files
on SHARED-SCRATCH volume):

| Streams | Agg read (MB/s) |
|---------|-----------------|
| 1       | 4,420           |
| 2       | 4,155           |
| 4       | 4,817           |
| 8       | 4,399           |
| 16      | 4,822           |

Bandwidth is **flat at 4.4-4.8 GB/s** across 1-16 streams. This is not
single-stream-limited — the bottleneck is elsewhere (likely NFS protocol or
Hammerspace server-side). This corrects the prior 1.5 GB/s figure. This
measurement is unaffected by the GPU clock issue.

Source: `data/raw/bw_shared_nfs_2026-09-12T11:15:22-05:00/summary.csv`

### KV cache size

Exact from model config, no measurement uncertainty:

    kv_bytes_per_token = 2 * n_layers * n_kv_heads * head_dim * sizeof(bf16)
                       = 2 * 32 * 8 * 128 * 2
                       = 131,072 bytes (128 KiB)

Full 128K-context KV cache: 16 GiB.

### Prefill scaling law shape

T_prefill(L) = a*L + b*L^2 fits with R^2 = 1.0 under both HF transformers and
vLLM (FlashAttention v3). The quadratic model is confirmed as the right
functional form. However, the measured coefficients (a = 1.306e-4, b = 3.727e-9
for HF; vLLM ~1.6x faster) are **lower bounds only** due to the clock issue
below.

## What is NOT established

### The crossover point

An initial crossover analysis concluded "fetching from NFS always beats
recomputing." This conclusion is invalid. It was computed using prefill
coefficients measured with SM clocks locked at 345 MHz (the GPU's minimum
frequency), which makes recompute appear ~5x slower than it would be at the
1785 MHz max boost clock. The crossover must be re-computed after clocks are
unlocked and prefill is re-measured.

## Blocking finding: SM clocks locked at minimum

All 4 H100 NVL GPUs have SM clocks pinned at 345 MHz (minimum supported) while
max boost is 1785 MHz. Evidence:

- nvidia-smi reports 345 MHz during sustained bf16 GEMM (100ms sampling,
  all 123 samples at 345 MHz while power draws 189W)
- Pure 16K^3 bf16 GEMM: 178.5 TFLOP/s = 95.7% of theoretical peak at 345 MHz
  (186.5 TFLOP/s for 132 SMs at 4096 flops/SM/clock)
- vLLM prefill at 32K: 152-155 TFLOP/s across all config variants tested
  (eager/compiled, chunked/unchunked, batch 1/4) — configs don't matter because
  the clock is the ceiling
- Power limit is 400W (above 310W default) — power is NOT the constraint
- No throttle reasons active other than GPU_IDLE between bursts
- `nvidia-smi -rgc` fails: user lacks permission to change clocks

**At full clocks, prefill speed would improve by a large but unquantified
factor. It must be re-measured, not extrapolated.** Clock speed affects compute
time linearly while compute-bound, but at higher clocks other bottlenecks
(memory bandwidth, interconnect) may become significant.

Evidence: `data/hw/clock_lock_evidence_2026-09-12T16:07:09-05:00.txt`

### Correction: H100 NVL peak TFLOP/s

The commonly cited "835 TFLOP/s" is wrong for our H100 NVL variant (132 SMs).
The correct theoretical bf16 Tensor peak at 1785 MHz is **965 TFLOP/s**,
derived from the H100 SXM spec (989.5 TFLOP/s at 1830 MHz with the same
132 SMs, 4096 flops/SM/clock).
